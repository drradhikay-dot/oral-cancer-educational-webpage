Oral Cancer Educational Web Resource

An interdisciplinary oral cancer informatics project integrating clinical dentistry, health informatics, and digital health communication.

Project Overview

This academic project was developed to organize and communicate evidence-based information about oral cancer through a structured web-based educational resource.

The project combines clinical knowledge from dentistry with health informatics and web content development, with emphasis on oral cancer risk factors, clinical presentation, staging, diagnosis, and treatment.

Introduction

Oral cancer includes malignancies affecting the lips, oral cavity, and related structures. Squamous cell carcinoma represents the predominant histological type of oral cancer.

Etiology and Risk Factors

Oral cancer has a multifactorial etiology involving behavioral, environmental, infectious, and genetic factors. Important risk factors include tobacco use, alcohol consumption, betel nut chewing, viral infections, radiation exposure, and genetic susceptibility.

Clinical Signs

Clinical findings associated with oral malignancy may include induration, fixation, persistent red or white patches, ulcerative lesions, tissue friability, and abnormal mucosal changes.

Symptoms

Symptoms may include persistent oral sores, pain or numbness, unexplained bleeding, difficulty chewing or swallowing, changes in tongue or jaw movement, cervical lymph node enlargement, weight loss, and persistent halitosis.

Staging

Oral cancer is commonly staged using the TNM system, which evaluates the primary tumor, regional lymph-node involvement, and distant metastasis.

Diagnosis and Early Detection

Early detection is important because diagnosis at an earlier stage may improve clinical outcomes. Diagnostic evaluation may include clinical examination, biopsy, imaging, and other diagnostic techniques.

Computed tomography and magnetic resonance imaging are commonly used in the evaluation and monitoring of head and neck malignancies.

Treatment

Management of oral cancer may involve surgery, radiotherapy, systemic therapy, targeted therapies, or combinations of these approaches depending on disease characteristics and stage.

Project Relevance

This project reflects an interdisciplinary interest in oral oncology, health informatics, digital health communication, early disease detection, and the organization of clinical information for educational and research applications.
